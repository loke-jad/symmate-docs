How It Works
The Registry
At its foundation, Sygil maintains a cryptographically secured identity registry for every member. Each member's identity is verified for unique personhood without requiring the disclosure of personal information to other members or to the platform's human operators. The registry uses zero-knowledge proofs to confirm that a member is a real, unique individual without revealing who they are.

Some attributes are public within the system: a handle, the date they joined, the goals they've committed to. Some are private, visible only to the member and their agent. Some are group-visible: track record on past commitments, vouching history, prediction accuracy. The registry tracks all of these as discrete, verifiable entries.

Identity verification happens through an in-person handshake model for the connection graph, establishing trust at the human level while maintaining cryptographic privacy at the system level. This isn't security plumbing bolted on after the fact. It's the load-bearing structure of the entire platform. Without it, Sygil would be just another fintech product with mutual aid branding. With it, the system enables a kind of collective intelligence that genuinely didn't exist before agentic infrastructure made it possible.

The Symmate Layer
Every member has a symmate — a persistent AI agent that operates as their standing representative within the system. The symmate observes the member's actual financial patterns: where money comes in, where it goes, what gets neglected, what gets prioritized, what patterns are stable, what represents recent disruption versus chronic instability. This is information no bank underwriter could purchase at any price. It's only available to the person living it, and now to their agent.

The symmate's relationship with its human is governed by an explicit consent framework. The symmate sees everything the member permits it to see, and the conditions under which it discloses anything to anyone — including the group — are explicit and revocable. Members define the granularity of their agent's autonomy: always ask before responding, answer from standing knowledge on familiar topics, or answer confidently on anything within established parameters. The member controls the boundary. The symmate operates within it.

When the symmate participates in group decisions on the member's behalf, the intelligence it carries gets aggregated into the group's collective view without ever leaving the agent's custody in attributable form. No individual member's financial situation, opinions, or history becomes visible to other members. The agent layer operates at full information. The human layer sees only the necessary surface.

This asymmetric reveal is the architectural key. Symmates negotiate with each other with complete context. One symmate can tell another that its member has a stable pattern and good track record and will fund a request, without either human ever learning who the other represents. Privacy is preserved at the human layer while coordination operates at the agent layer.

The Group Mind
Any member can query the collective intelligence of the group. When a question is posed — whether it's a capital allocation decision, a request for advice, a prediction about an outcome, or a simple sentiment check — every symmate responds from what it knows about its human. The responses return aggregated and anonymous. No individual answer is attributable to any individual person. The result is a distribution, not a list of opinions with names attached.

Critically, symmates vote. Humans don't — not by default. If a human has an opinion they want to make known, they can surface it as a separate signal through a separate channel with separate weight. But the default state is the symmate speaking on behalf of the member's standing position, informed by everything it knows about them.

This solves several problems simultaneously. It removes the noise of bad days and emotional reactivity. It eliminates social pressure to perform an opinion. It allows members who don't have a strong view to stay out without their abstention skewing the result. The symmate vote represents the considered, contextual position of the member. The human voice represents live intent when they care enough to express it directly. Two distinct signals serving two distinct purposes.

The polling mechanism supports multiple modes of inquiry. Simple sentiment polling lets members ask questions like whether a job offer is worth taking or whether a lease looks reasonable. The group answers honestly because responses are anonymous, and the asker receives an aggregated read that's more honest than what any individual member would offer face to face. Forward-looking prediction aggregation lets members ask whether they'll regret a decision in six months, with responses weighted by each symmate's track record on past predictions. Market sentiment aggregation lets members ask how many people have used a particular service, recommended it, or regretted it, creating a trusted internal review system without the distortions of public platforms.

This group mind is a fundamentally different epistemic product than asking a large language model for advice. When you query an LLM, you receive a confident answer assembled from training data with no actual stake in your life. When you query the Sygil network, you receive an answer from actual humans — or rather, from agents that know those humans intimately — who have real context and real reputational skin in being right. The LLMs are guessing. The network is knowing, in aggregate.

The collective intelligence isn't a feature. It's the core primitive. Capital allocation decisions, trust assessments, contractor evaluations, financial guidance — all of them route through the same aggregation mechanism, all return anonymous distributions, all benefit from reputation weighting and differential privacy protections.
